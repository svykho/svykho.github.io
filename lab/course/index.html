Black-hole perturbation theory

From a leaky oscillator to the ringing of real black holes, in twelve short lessons.

How it works

When a black hole is disturbed, by a merger or by anything that falls in, it rings like a bell and settles down. Black-hole perturbation theory is the art of describing that ringing with small deformations of the geometry, and it is how we read the end of every gravitational-wave signal.

Each lesson tells the story first, with a figure you can play with, and keeps the full calculation folded away until you want it. Every number on these pages was computed for the course, most of them live in your browser. Start with Why perturb a black hole, or jump to any lesson below.

You need
Waves, complex numbers and some calculus. General relativity helps but is not required.
Units
G = c = 1, with lengths and times measured in units of the black-hole mass M.
Each lesson
A story, live figures, the full calculation, one exercise and further reading.
